Mercury Retrograde is a hand-built [nixie tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixie_tube) alarm clock that is controlled, set and manipulated using a 3-axis accelerometer. The clock is an exploration of the role of intentionality in everyday objects, as well as an experiment in [resistentialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistentialism) behavior. In order to function correctly, it demands physical attention. Ignore it for too long and it begins to rebel, intentionally slowing its internal oscillations and waking its owner at inconvenient hours of the night.

Originally a first-year master’s degree project for UC Irvine’s Arts Computation Engineering program, the clock now sits, faithfully ticking the seconds away, on my desk.

## Features

- Clock and alarm set through simple shake and rotate gestures
- An alarm with programmable melodies
- Audible alerts during mode changes
- A backup power supply
- A case of plexiglass, red oak and walnut

The microcontroller code and schematics for the clock are [on GitHub](https://github.com/svoisen/mercury_retrograde).
